# Retrieval

What retrieval failures, search methods, and later retrieval patterns does the course state?

- About 90% of RAG systems fail in production, and he says they fail for the same reasons (about 00:00:00 and again about 01:33:18). A system that works on about 10 documents can break at about 10,000.
- The course order he states: foundation (documents, chunks, embeddings, stores); five failure modes; quality optimization; scale; a full system; then agentic RAG, GraphRAG, contextual retrieval, and multimodal RAG (about 00:00:00).
- Indexing is once: load, split (example 500 to 1,000 tokens), embed, store vectors with the original text. Query path every time: embed the question with the same model, search, fetch text, augment the prompt, call the chat model (about 00:55:04). Mixing embedding models breaks comparison.
- Garbage embeddings mean garbage retrieval and garbage answers. A stronger chat model does not repair bad retrieval. Embedding quality beats embedding more documents. Test retrieval separately. About 90 percent of RAG failures are retrieval failures, not generation failures (about 00:58:29).
- Five failure modes (about 01:33:18): bad chunking, embedding mismatch or semantic drift, retrieval noise, context overflow, and hallucination even when the answer is in the context.
- Vector search fails on strings with no semantics: product codes and SKUs, unknown acronyms (WCAG example), exact personal names, and error codes. BM25 counts terms and does the opposite: strong on exact rare tokens, weak on synonyms (about 01:53:39).
- Hybrid search splits the query to vector search and BM25, then reciprocal rank fusion. Documents that rank well in both rise. Use it for enterprise codes, technical docs, legal docs, and mixed queries when accuracy matters. Do not use it when latency is the whole point (about 01:58:33).
- Equal weights are the start. 0.3/0.7 leans semantic; 0.7/0.3 leans toward codes. Tune from query logs and watch which retriever contributed (about 02:05:25). Fusion is not a simple merge of the two lists (about 02:08:47).
- BM25 must be rebuilt on each document add. Suggested k about 4. Hybrid adds about 20 to 50 ms (about 02:12:09).
- Parent-document retrieval searches the child and returns the parent so embeddings stay specific and the model still sees the section (about 02:52:25).
- Contextual compression asks the model which sentences answer the query. Benefits he lists: fewer tokens (about 85 percent in the noisy demo), less distraction, faster generation. Cost: extra LLM calls at retrieval. Worth it for large mixed documents and when token cost dominates (about 02:59:10).
- Multi-query, contextual compression, BM25, and parent-document classes were imported from `langchain_classic` in this recording because packages had moved. He says classic may be deprecated by the end of 2026 (about 03:02:36).
- Contextual retrieval prepends model-written context before embedding. Anthropic figures he quotes: about 49 percent fewer top-20 failures alone, about 67 percent with reranking. One-time index cost about 1 to 5 cents per document. Chunks grow about 20 to 30 percent (about 06:15:05).
- An agent, in his definition, uses an LLM, calls tools, and makes decisions. Agentic RAG routes, grades, rewrites, retries, and can switch retrieval tools. Traditional RAG is one shot (about 06:38:03).
- Max retries are mandatory or the loop spends tokens forever. The fallback must be a normal answer, not an exception. He calls the grade-rewrite-retrieve loop the production pattern for 2026 onward (about 06:56:42). Use agentic RAG for reformulation, high stakes, mixed document types, and user-facing apps; not for a toy demo (about 07:03:26).
- Text extraction destroys tables, charts, and layout. ColPali embeds page images. The query model must match the document model. A vision LLM reads the retrieved image. About 10 times the cost of text RAG in his ballpark (about 1 cent versus about 10 cents a query). Needs a GPU or a cloud GPU, PDF rendering, and a store that accepts those vectors. Keep a text fallback (about 07:24:28).
- His RAG timeline (about 07:37:02): 2023 is naive chunk, embed, retrieve, generate; 2024 adds hybrid search and reranking; 2025 adds contextual retrieval and self-correction; 2026 onward is agentic, multimodal, and graph-enhanced. Minimum bar he states for systems built now: contextual retrieval, reranking, agentic patterns, and multimodal support. He says the naive chunk-and-pray strategy is dead (about 07:38:34).
